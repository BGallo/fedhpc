# Where FedMIP trails Gurobi: a technical analysis

This analysis uses the direct comparison in [`direct_2026-11-27_60s.csv`](direct_2026-11-27_60s.csv) and its raw [`JSONL`](direct_2026-11-27_60s.jsonl). It covers all 240 instances of the MIPLIB 2017 Benchmark Set and 21 Yu et al. UPMSP instances (one representative from each jobs-by-machines size cell). The Yu archive has 420 instances; conclusions about it are preliminary until the remaining replicates are run.

Both solvers read the same MPS formulation, ran single-threaded with a 60-second limit and relative gap target 1e-4. Gurobi was 13.0.1. FedMIP's report-fix reruns replaced 15 older MIPLIB records where a validated incumbent had been dropped on a root-LP timeout. A time-limit result is not a proof of poor ultimate performance; these are bounded experiments, and the benchmark set itself was selected for diversity and solvability by the union of solvers. [MIPLIB describes that selection process here](https://miplib.zib.de/Selection_Methodology.html).

## Executive result

| Suite | FedMIP reached target | Gurobi reached target | FedMIP found a feasible incumbent | Gurobi found a feasible incumbent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MIPLIB 2017 (240) | 18 (7.5%) | 108 (45.0%) | 144 (60.0%) | 221 (92.1%) |
| Yu UPMSP subset (21) | 0 | 8 (38.1%) | 19 (90.5%) | 21 (100%) |

On MIPLIB, both found an incumbent on 143 cases. On those, Gurobi's final objective was strictly better on 117, equal on 21, and worse on 5. On Yu, both found an incumbent on 19 cases; Gurobi's final objective was better on all 19. The latter result is striking because FedMIP's *first* incumbent was better on 14 of those 19 cases. The main Yu weakness is therefore not simply finding an initial feasible schedule: it is time to a first solution on large cases and, especially, improving the incumbent afterward.

## Where the largest gaps occur

### Largest MIPLIB quality gap: `glass4` (nesting)

The largest absolute final-objective difference among paired feasible MIPLIB runs is `glass4`:

| Metric | FedMIP | Gurobi |
|---|---:|---:|
| Incumbent | 2,000,017,700 | 1,200,012,600 |
| Best bound at stop | 800,006,165 | 1,200,010,300 |
| Nodes | 15,332 | 31,839 |
| Time | 60.12 s | 59.19 s |
| First incumbent | 3,650,030,000 at 5.40 s | 3,133,355,917 at 0.005 s |
| Incumbent updates | 6 | 30 |

Gurobi reached the official best-known value and its configured gap target. The FedMIP incumbent is 66.7% above Gurobi's value, and its final bound is far weaker. FedMIP added 661 cuts, but its recorded root bound moved only from about 800.002M to 800.004M. This is evidence of difficulty extracting useful bound improvement from the current root-cut/search pipeline on this model. `glass4` is a nesting instance with 322 variables, 396 constraints, and 1,815 nonzeros; its model is small, so raw size does not explain the gap. The official page identifies it as a nesting instance and records 1,200,012,600 as a feasible known solution. [MIPLIB `glass4` details](https://miplib.zib.de/instance_details_glass4.html).

The objective magnitude makes *absolute* differences look large; the normalized 66.7% incumbent gap and the bound shortfall make this a real search/relaxation gap, not just a scale artifact. The run record says Gurobi reached its 1e-4 target in 59.2 seconds, not that the two solvers had equal time to quality.

Other notable MIPLIB incumbent gaps include:

- `trento1`: FedMIP 17.44M versus Gurobi 5.19M; first FedMIP incumbent took 30.6 s, while Gurobi's arrived in 0.0033 s. FedMIP's root bound is about 5.183M and Gurobi's final bound is about 5.189M. Those similar end bounds, alongside very different incumbents, point more strongly to primal heuristic/search quality than to bound strength.
- `co-100`: FedMIP 10.66M versus Gurobi 2.65M, although FedMIP's root bound and Gurobi's final bound are both near 0.92M. This is another case where a relatively weak incumbent dominates the gap.
- `sorrell3`: FedMIP -10 versus Gurobi -15, with FedMIP bound -512 versus Gurobi -20. The sign and near-zero objectives make percentage-gap rankings misleading; compare the objectives and bounds directly.
- `leo2`, `sp97ar`, and `sp98ar`: Gurobi incumbents are about 2.4–7.1% better, with bounds also somewhat stronger. In these large-scale objective models, FedMIP spends a large fraction of the limit in LP work.

### Largest Yu scheduling gap: 200-job cases

The most informative repeated pattern is at 200 jobs. For `16_200_TWCT_1`, FedMIP found 167,140 after 43.6 s and made no later update; Gurobi's first incumbent was worse (609,961) but it improved to 84,781, about 49% of FedMIP's final value. For `20_200_TWCT_1`, FedMIP found 122,497 after 52.6 s; Gurobi went from 519,906 at 0.184 s to 64,210 by the limit, 47.6% below FedMIP. FedMIP's initial solution can be competitive, but its incumbent improvement stalls.

At 300 jobs the gap is more fundamental. FedMIP found no incumbent on either 16- or 20-machine representatives. Gurobi found incumbents on both: 175,629 and 136,869 respectively. These are still time-limit outcomes, not proofs of optimality. The 20×300 MPS has 903,000 variables, 2,691,300 constraints, and 6,285,000 nonzeros. FedMIP's recorded stage times for 20×300 include about 14.2 s in root LP, 32.1 s in clique construction, 7.3 s in root cuts, and 6.1 s in RENS; the run reaches the deadline without producing a schedule. This makes clique generation/root processing a high-priority target for this formulation.

These models encode the exact assignment MILP for weighted completion time after fixing each machine's within-machine order by Smith's rule. They contain binary assignment variables and continuous McCormick variables for job-pair co-assignment. The pair terms grow quadratically with job count. That is a particularly revealing stress test for LP cost, clique processing, and primal heuristics. The model conversion and data source are documented in the [Yu et al. repository](https://github.com/INFORMSJoC/2024.0704) and [paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2024.0704).

## What kinds of problems are harder for FedMIP?

### 1. Large assignment/pairwise formulations

The Yu runs are the clearest problem class where FedMIP loses ground as size grows. Time to first incumbent rises from fractions of a second on many 40–80-job models to 33–53 seconds on 200-job cases; the two 300-job cases have no incumbent. Gurobi's first incumbents remain below half a second even on 300 jobs, and its final solution improves sharply after that first point. The combination of quadratic pair variables, hundreds of thousands to millions of rows, and expensive clique preprocessing overwhelms FedMIP before a useful branch-and-bound phase can develop.

### 2. Models where root LP or node LP work dominates

For MIPLIB, 55 FedMIP cases spent more than 50 seconds in the root LP and ended with zero branch nodes; 16 of those did have a feasible incumbent. Across the whole suite, 88 runs ended with zero nodes. This indicates a root-processing bottleneck, not just an undersized tree search. On `air05`, for example, both solvers found the same optimum, but FedMIP took 50.7 s versus Gurobi's 5.1 s. FedMIP recorded 208,829 simplex iterations and 43.6 LP seconds; Gurobi recorded 59,415 iterations. On `neos-827175`, both found the same optimum, but FedMIP used 42.1 s and 54,699 iterations while Gurobi used 0.7 s and 20,976 iterations. These point to LP throughput, hot-start/basis reuse, and per-node overhead as improvement areas.

### 3. Difficult primal search even when the initial bound is similar

`glass4`, `trento1`, and `co-100` show that comparable or not-too-distant FedMIP root bounds do not guarantee competitive incumbents. FedMIP's current heuristic portfolio can return a feasible point but may fail to improve it often enough. For `glass4`, Gurobi had 30 incumbent updates while FedMIP had 6. For `trento1`, FedMIP's first point came after 30.6 s and remained far above Gurobi's final incumbent. This suggests the incumbent-finding and improvement phases need separate attention from cut generation.

The benchmark does **not** justify saying that every class or every MIPLIB family is uniformly hard for FedMIP. MIPLIB is intentionally broad and not balanced as a scheduling benchmark. The named examples above are diagnoses of observed cases, not statistically controlled class-wide conclusions.

## Why Gurobi can win even when final objective quality is close

The clearest example is `air05`: both return objective 26,374 and reach the requested gap target, yet FedMIP needs about 10× the time. FedMIP explores fewer nodes (361 versus 903), but node counts are not comparable units across solvers. It uses more than 3× as many recorded LP iterations, which indicates that its LP/node work is costlier here. On `app1-1`, FedMIP explores only 40 nodes versus Gurobi's 1,132 and both prove objective -3, yet Gurobi is still about 3.3× faster. This reinforces that fewer nodes do not mean less total work.

Gurobi's advantage is consistent with stronger presolve and reductions, more effective cut selection, better LP warm starts, and a more mature portfolio of primal heuristics, diving, and branching. Those mechanisms are plausible explanations, not individually proven by this experiment: the current harness records Gurobi's final bound, work, iterations, solution count, and incumbent events, but it does not capture its root bound, presolve reductions, cut counts, or per-stage times. A follow-up instrumented run should capture those before attributing a particular win to a named Gurobi feature.

On Yu, the early incumbent data adds nuance: FedMIP's first incumbent beats Gurobi's first on 14/19 shared-feasible cases, but Gurobi eventually beats FedMIP's final objective on all 19. Thus Gurobi's advantage is not uniformly “finds the first feasible solution better”; it is “finds one very quickly, then continues improving.” In the 200-job cases, that follow-through creates most of the final gap.

## Correctness and measurement caveats

1. **Three MIPLIB feasibility contradictions are blockers for performance claims.** FedMIP says infeasible while Gurobi returns an incumbent on `momentum1`, `neos-3656078-kumeu`, and `satellites2-60-fs`. These need independent candidate validation and presolve/MPS-reader investigation. A feasible candidate from Gurobi is evidence against FedMIP's infeasibility status, but the harness did not save the candidate vectors for a separate checker.
2. **Fifteen FedMIP feasible results have no finite bound.** They occurred when root LP stopped at its deadline. The incumbent-reporting defect is fixed, but a primal-only time-limit result cannot support a gap comparison.
3. **Relative gaps near zero or for negative objectives are unstable to interpretation.** The report's absolute objective and bound are the safer metrics in such cases. `sorrell3` illustrates this.
4. **The Yu result is representative, not exhaustive.** It uses one seed per size cell; random instance variability has not been measured.
5. **One seed and short time limits are not a production benchmark.** Solver versions, machine/load state, and random seeds can change ordering and outcomes. The run used one thread but scheduled six pinned jobs at once. Repeat the high-impact instances serially with fixed seeds before publication.
6. **`optimal` is relative to the configured tolerance.** It means the solver stopped at the requested 1e-4 gap target; do not read it as an exact proof unless the reported bound and incumbent establish that separately.

## Highest-value next work

1. Fix and independently validate the three infeasibility contradictions first; add a candidate-solution export/check path to the harness.
2. Profile and cap/adapt clique construction on the Yu formulation. The 20×300 run spends about half its budget there. Run feasible schedule heuristics before expensive global clique scans and test whether a formulation-aware assignment heuristic can produce a valid incumbent early.
3. Improve incumbent *updates*, not only first-incumbent discovery: compare root diving, RINS/RENS, feasibility pump/jump, and neighborhood repair by time-to-best-objective on `glass4`, `trento1`, `co-100`, and Yu 200-job cases.
4. Profile fedlp on `air05`, `neos-827175`, `glass4`, and the large Yu models. Separate root LP, node LP, and strong-branching time; measure basis reuse and iteration throughput against Gurobi.
5. Instrument Gurobi for root relaxation bounds, presolve size reductions, cut counts, and progress logs. Then re-run matched cases with several seeds and report performance profiles, not only suite averages.
6. Expand Yu to all 420 instances after cost controls are in place. Preserve the representative subset as the smoke/profile suite; do not extrapolate it to the full size cell replication distribution.
